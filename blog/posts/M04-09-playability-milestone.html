Playable, at 14% of one core

The milestone that proves the emulator core: run the first act of each game at full speed. The automated parts passed easily. The human part is still waiting for a human.

genesisEmu build notes3 min readStory M04-09

M00 to M04 build the emulator, and M06 onward builds the reverse-engineering workbench on top of it. M04-09 is the checkpoint between the two: before investing in debuggers and ROM maps, show that the core actually runs the target games well. Concretely, the first act of each game, Green Hill Zone 1, Emerald Hill Zone 1 and Angel Island Zone 1, playable at full speed (without sound, which was M05's job).

The story has three acceptance criteria. Two are checked. One is honestly open.

Scripted stand-ins for a player

The agent that implemented the milestone couldn't hold a controller, so it played the games through the scripted-input format from M04-03, and checked dumped frames by eye at chosen points:

  • Sonic 1: Start on the title at frame 620, then run right with jumps. Frame 1,150 shows Sonic running in Green Hill act 1 at 0:05 on the game clock, next to a Motobug. At frame 1,300 he's knocked back on the bridge in the hurt pose, with rings scattered. Collision and damage work.
  • Sonic 2: Start at frame 950, because earlier presses during the title animation are ignored by the game. Frame 1,500 shows one-player Emerald Hill act 1 at 0:06, Sonic mid-jump, Tails following.
  • Sonic 3 & Knuckles: Start on the title, "no save" on the data-select screen, then right and jumps after the Angel Island intro. Frame 2,400 shows Sonic in act 1 at 0:06 with a score of 100 and 7 rings.

Those runs are the third criterion, a scripted-input regression for each act start. Their checkpoints hold frame and state hashes, and the [rom] test replays them.

The whole app, emulation, UI and video upload together, used about 14% of one core at 60 frames per second.

Performance: no optimisation needed

The second criterion was a budget: at most 25% of one CPU core at 60 fps on the reference machine, without instrumentation. The numbers (Windows 11, Intel i7-11700KF, Release build):

MeasurementResult
Core, per emulated frame0.55–0.75 ms
Scripted runs via gen_cli (S1 / S2 / S3K)0.69 / 0.63 / 0.72 ms per frame
Whole app: emulation + ImGui + OpenGL upload, including start-up2.4 ms CPU per frame, about 14% of one core

A frame at 60 Hz lasts 16.7 ms. The core uses well under a millisecond of it, which leaves a lot of room for the instrumentation that M06 and M07 will add on every memory access. That headroom is why M01-11 pushed for a fast CPU core in the first place. The story's scope included "performance profiling and fixes"; the profiling showed no fixes were needed.

Bug triage

The scope also asked for a list of visual issues found while playing. From the sampled frames, the list is empty. One item was noted as worth a look by eye. During the Sonic 3 & Knuckles data-select fade-in (around frame 820 of the script), a frame or two shows a half-built screen. The story's judgement is that this is probably what the hardware shows while the screen is loading, but it's written down rather than dismissed.

The open box

The first criterion reads: GHZ1, EHZ1 and AIZ1 completed by a human without visible glitches. A script that runs right and jumps isn't a human finishing an act. It doesn't chase rings, take alternate paths or reach the boss, so it can't stand in for one. The box stays unchecked and the story says "Implemented; human playtest pending (Chris)". The pull request lists it under verification gaps, together with the gamepad hot-plug test from M04-02 and the macOS build.

That's consistent with how the project works throughout. A criterion is checked only when it has actually been verified, and a near-substitute is recorded as a near-substitute. Two milestones of automated evidence say the games run correctly. Whether they play correctly needs someone to pick up a pad.
