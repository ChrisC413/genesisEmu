Interrupts on the right line

Underwater palettes, split-screen effects and status bars all depend on interrupts firing at the right scanline. Here's how genesisEmu lines up the 68000, the VDP and the beam.

genesisEmu build notes3 min readStory M03-03

The Genesis draws its picture one scanline at a time, top to bottom, and games can change VDP state while it draws. Change the palette halfway down and the bottom half of the screen uses new colours. That is how the water in Sonic games gets its tint: the game switches to an underwater palette exactly at the water surface. To do that on the right line, the game needs an interrupt on the right line. M03-03 made those interrupts precise.

The order of events in a line

Every line, the scheduler from M02-05 does the same three things: Vdp::beginLine, run the 68000 for the line's 3,420 master clocks, then Vdp::endLine. Where each VDP event sits in that sequence decides which line a change affects.

Because each line is rendered at its end, after the CPU has run for it, a CRAM write the CPU makes during line N shows from line N on. A unit test, Mid-frame CRAM writes land on the following lines, pins this behaviour down.

The H-int counter

The horizontal interrupt is driven by a counter. The game loads register 10 with a value N. The VDP decrements the counter after each active line, and when it underflows it raises H-int and reloads the counter from register 10. During vertical blank the counter is simply reloaded. The result is an interrupt every N+1 lines, starting at a line the game controls:

A water effect sets register 10 so the counter underflows on the line just above the surface. The handler then runs on the surface line and swaps the palette, and the rest of the frame is underwater.

The underwater palette starts exactly at the water surface. One line off, and anyone who has played the game would notice.

V-int and the Z80

At the first vertical-blank line (224 in the usual mode), beginLine sets the V-int pending flag. If register 1 enables it, the 68000 gets a level-6 interrupt. The pending flag also appears in the status register, and a test checks that enabling V-int late, after the flag is already set, still fires the interrupt.

The same line raises the Z80's interrupt input for one line. The Z80 has only one interrupt, and sound drivers typically use it as a once-per-frame tick. In M03 nothing consumed it yet, because the Z80 didn't run, but the signal was exposed as VdpState::z80Int for M04.

HV counter tables

M02-04 introduced the HV counter with its NTSC jumps. M03-03 completed the tables: vertical counts for 224- and 240-line modes on NTSC and PAL, horizontal counts for the 256- and 320-pixel modes, and both interlace modes, where the vertical counter doubles and shows bit 8 in its lowest bit. Each variant has a unit test with expected values at the jump points.

Verified, and not verified

The story's headline criterion, Labyrinth Zone's water line in Sonic 1 appears at the right height, is not checked. Labyrinth Zone isn't part of Sonic 1's attract demos, so reaching it needs controller input, which arrived in M04. The same mechanism was checked instead on Sonic 2's Aquatic Ruin Zone attract demo. The underwater palette begins exactly at the water surface, and that frame (8,000) is one of the golden checkpoints in M03-11. The unit tests cover the counter sequences: H-int every N+1 lines including line 224, reload in V-blank, V-int and the Z80 interrupt for one line, and acknowledgement.
