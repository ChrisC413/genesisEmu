Who's in front: priority and shadow/highlight

Sonic runs in front of a loop's back half and behind its front half, using a single bit per tile. The same bit, in another mode, darkens and brightens the screen.

genesisEmu build notes4 min readStory M03-08

The Genesis has no depth buffer. Its idea of "in front" is simpler: every tile and every sprite has one priority bit. That's enough for one of the most memorable effects in the series. In a Green Hill loop, Sonic passes in front of one part of the loop and behind another, because the level's art marks some tiles high-priority and the game changes Sonic's own priority bit as he goes round.

M03-08 implemented the composition rules, and the shadow/highlight mode that reuses the same bit for lighting effects.

Shadow and highlight

Setting bit 3 of register 12 turns on shadow/highlight mode, and the priority bit gains a second meaning. Pixels where neither plane has priority are drawn darker (shadow). That gives games a cheap way to darken parts of the screen: mark the tiles low-priority. Sprites can then act as light sources and shadows. In palette line 3, colour 14 doesn't draw. It highlights whatever is beneath. Colour 15 shadows it.

The rules have a few exceptions, and the composer implements them in about fifteen lines:

  • A pixel is shadowed when neither plane's priority bit is set, even if those plane pixels are transparent. This is why M03-04 kept the priority bit on transparent pixel codes.
  • A highlight operator on a shadowed pixel brings it back to normal; on a normal pixel it brightens it.
  • High-priority sprite pixels are never shaded, and colour 14 of palettes 0–2 is always drawn at normal intensity.

The three intensities map onto the 15-step colour ramp from M03-04: shadow uses steps 0–7, normal the even steps, and highlight steps 7–14. All three versions of every CRAM colour are precomputed in the palette cache, so shading costs nothing extra per pixel.

One bit per tile decides whether Sonic runs in front of the loop or behind it.

Debug filters

The layer mask from M03-06 includes a priority filter: keep only high-priority pixels or only low ones. For a reverse engineer this shows at a glance which tiles a level designer marked as foreground, the information that decides what Sonic can pass behind.

Verified, partly

Unit tests check the full priority order (B low < A low < sprite low < B high < A high < sprite high) and the shadow/highlight rules: plane-priority shading, both operators, high-priority sprites and colour 14. These replaced the test ROM the story originally asked for.

The loop criterion is only partly checked. In Sonic 1's Green Hill attract demo at frame 2,800, Sonic is drawn between the back of the loop and the grass in front of it, as expected. Passing behind the loop's front wasn't followed frame by frame, so the story is still "In progress". Shadow/highlight was verified only by the synthetic tests.
