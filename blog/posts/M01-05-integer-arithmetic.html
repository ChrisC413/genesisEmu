Arithmetic, flags and the price of a division

Adding two numbers is easy. Producing the same five condition flags as the 68000, and taking exactly as long to divide, is the hard part.

genesisEmu build notes5 min readStory M01-05

Game code is full of arithmetic. Positions plus velocities, health minus damage, a compare and a branch in every if. M01-05 covers the 68000's integer arithmetic: the ADD and SUB families with their address, extended and quick variants, CMP and CMPA, NEG and NEGX, EXT, and the multiply and divide instructions MULU, MULS, DIVU and DIVS.

The result was 100% of 266,145 SingleStepTests vectors across 33 files, including the cycle counts of multiply and divide, which depend on the values being processed.

One function for every add and subtract

After an add or subtract the 68000 sets five condition flags: C (carry or borrow), V (signed overflow), Z (zero), N (negative) and X (extend, a copy of carry kept for multi-precision maths). Rather than computing them separately in each handler, genesisEmu uses a single templated function, arith<S, O>, for every add, subtract and compare at every size.

It widens the operation to 64 bits, so the carry is simply the bit just above the operand size. Signed overflow uses the textbook trick: an add overflows when both inputs have the same sign and the result's sign differs.

CMP is a subtract that throws its result away. In the same function it simply skips updating X, which compares never touch.

The sticky Z flag

ADDX, SUBX and NEGX exist for arithmetic wider than 32 bits: you add the low longs, then the high longs with the carry from X. For the Z flag to mean "the whole 64-bit result is zero", these instructions never set Z. They only clear it when a part is non-zero. The programmer sets Z before the chain, and any non-zero part clears it for good.

On a 68000, how long a multiply takes depends on the bits of the number you multiply by.

Multiplication timing depends on the data

The 68000 multiplies with a shift-and-add loop in microcode. Each step that adds costs extra time, so the cycle count depends on the bits of the source operand. For MULU it's 38 cycles plus 2 per set bit. For MULS it's 38 plus 2 per change between adjacent bits of the source, with a zero appended below bit 0:

Multiplying by $FFFF is therefore 32 cycles slower than multiplying by 0. Over a frame those differences add up and shift where interrupts land, which is why the project insists on exact counts.

Division: simulate the microcode loop

Division is worse: 76 to 136 cycles for DIVU, 120 to 158 for DIVS, depending on the bits of the quotient as it's produced. No closed formula captures it neatly, so the core simulates the hardware's restoring-division loop just to count the cycles, then computes the actual result with ordinary C++ division.

Where the manual is silent

Motorola's manual leaves some flag results "undefined". Real chips still produce something, and the test suite records it. The story notes list the behaviour adopted from the suite:

  • When a division overflows (the quotient doesn't fit in 16 bits), V is set, C is cleared, and N and Z keep their previous values. The destination register is left unchanged.
  • DIVS detects overflow before its loop when the absolute quotient is at least $8000, so a quotient of exactly −32,768, which would fit in a signed word, still reports overflow, after (8 or 9) × 2 cycles.
  • Division by zero raises the zero-divide exception. The suite has a single such vector (DIVU (d16, A7)). It stacks the address of the DIV instruction itself and takes 38 cycles plus EA time, and the core follows it.
  • ADDQ.L/SUBQ.L #,An takes 6 cycles, per the suite, not the 8 that some tables list.

Verified

The 33 files cover ADD, ADDA, ADDX, SUB, SUBA, SUBX, CMP, CMPA, NEG and NEGX in all sizes (the suite files them together with their immediate, quick and memory forms), plus EXT.w/l, MULU, MULS, DIVU and DIVS. All 266,145 vectors pass with exact cycles. The arithmetic Sonic does every frame, such as adding sub-pixel velocities and comparing positions against level boundaries, now runs on a core that matches the reference in both result and timing.
